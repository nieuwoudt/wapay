# OTT Collect — decision analysis (2026-08-25)

*40-agent multi-lens review of the actual agreement text (operational, commercial,
regulatory, strategic), each finding adversarially challenged against the contract.
Verdict: **DEFER — do not sign this paper; chase the digital variant.***

## The finding that decides it

**Reading B — crediting the Consumer's WaPay wallet instead of paying cash — is
EXPRESSLY FORBIDDEN, not merely unaddressed.** All four lenses independently reached
this, and the challenge agents could not break it:

- **16.3**: "It is noted that the Consumer may only be paid out in cash and in South
  African Currency. The money paid out to the Consumer must not be counterfeit."
  "Only" is exclusionary; the counterfeit sentence proves banknotes are meant.
- **1.9**: Redeem means redeeming a Voucher "in exchange for cash" — and **2.1.12**
  makes definitions substantively enforceable, so the cash requirement is load-bearing
  in the definition every operative clause hangs off (appointment 4.1, licence 5.1,
  consideration 9.1, fee 9.5).
- **1.3** Consumer "presents to the Representative's terminal base"; **16.2** vendor
  cash floats; **11.1** branding "erected at or near the points of sale"; **25.1.3**
  OTT may terminate immediately if the Representative "is regularly unable to perform
  cash outs as determined by OTT in its sole discretion".

WaPay has no premises, no vendors, no cash. Clause 11.1 alone is unperformable — there
is no surface to erect branding on.

## ⚠️ Do NOT sign it as a dormant "filing cabinet" contract

**17.4** warrants the Representative "is not aware of anything which might or will
adversely affect its ability to perform its obligations". WaPay knows it has no cash
and no premises. **24.2.4 / 25.2.4** let OTT terminate immediately for intentional or
negligent misrepresentation, and **25.1.7** claim damages. Signing-but-not-performing
converts a dormant contract into a live misrepresentation claim OTT could assert as
leverage — including on the Payout agreement WaPay has now signed.

## Why it is still worth one email

The prize is real: **it replaces cash-in cost, not fee income.** Loading R500 via
PayFast costs ~R20.70 (4.14%). The same R500 arriving as an Instant Money redemption
would EARN ~R1.19 blended. And the float direction inverts — under Reading A WaPay
fronts cash and waits for reimbursement (negative working capital, and **9.2 sets no
T+n, no deadline, no interest**); under Reading B WaPay receives value first.

Fees (Annexure A 5.11): Standard Bank **R0.75 incl VAT**, Nedbank **R3.00 excl VAT**,
VodaPay **1% excl VAT, uncapped** (the best line, ~13x Standard Bank on the same
ticket). 5.11.1 is garbled ("per Month") — the rate table needs clarifying. On the fee
alone this is not a business; 100% of the value is in Reading B.

Also confirmed benign: non-exclusivity is mutual (4.2, 5.2), no volume commitment or
take-or-pay anywhere, either party may terminate on 1 month (6.2). Annexure A 5.1 bars
charging the Consumer — under Reading B that is the marketing headline, not a problem.

## The question that actually decides it

**Is "cash only" OTT's rule, or a Standard Bank / Nedbank / Vodacom scheme rule flowing
down through OTT?** (16.8 and 16.10 subordinate the Representative to risk and
certification requirements imposed by Regulatory Authorities and OTT.) If it is a bank
mandate, an OTT side letter is worthless — it protects WaPay against OTT and nobody
else. **Get the bank position, not just OTT's.**

## Regulatory note (counsel, not us)

Even amended, Collect makes WaPay a bank-facing redemption agent with FICA exposure,
three uncapped indemnities, no regulatory umbrella from OTT, and a continuing warranty
(**17.8**) that its operations comply with all applicable laws — no knowledge
qualifier. Crediting a wallet from a bank voucher may read as e-money issuance. This
belongs in the same counsel brief as payouts.
