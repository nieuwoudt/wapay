## 150. "I want an invite link" (21 October, 09:13 SAST) has no reply on the customer's phone

- **Symptom:** the customer's message on 21 October at 09:13 SAST (07:13 UTC) shows no answer in her WhatsApp.
- **Root cause (from the record, to confirm against `processed_messages`):** routing is fine: `matchInviteAsk` (`lib/referrals.js`) catches the sentence and `handleInviteAsk` replies. 07:13 UTC falls inside Meta's 30-day "Sending spam" restriction (BUGLOG #136, 20 October 21:30 UTC to 21 October 14:35 UTC), when every reply was accepted by the API and then failed with 131031 on the phone. WaPay recorded a reply that never arrived. When deliveries resumed, nobody went back to the messages that had been dropped.
- **Status: OPEN.** The cause is #136, and its guard (`lib/delivery-health.js`) now shows the stall. The gap that remains is re-answering: when deliveries resume, nothing lists the customers whose last reply failed so a person can answer them. Rule 4.6 still applies: only inside each customer's own 24-hour window, or by a person.

## 149. The wallet PIN typed at the chat's PIN step stays in the customer's own WhatsApp history

- **Symptom:** the customer typed her WaPay PIN as a chat reply at the PIN step of an earlier withdrawal (the R325 PayShap, reference `WPED6D478285A1E9`). The PIN is in her WhatsApp history, in any phone backup, and in the screenshots she shared.
- **Root cause:** this has been the design since v1. Every money flow ends at "type your PIN" in chat (golden thread 2.1). WaPay never stores or logs it: `redactForMemory`, the model boundary and log masking all hold. But nothing tells the customer that the message stays on their phone, and nothing asks them to delete it. The WhatsApp Cloud API cannot delete a customer's own message.
- **Fix, interim (CHANGELOG 134):** the withdrawal PIN step adds `PIN_PRIVACY_LINE`: "WaPay never keeps your PIN. After you send it, you can delete that message: press and hold it, then *Delete for me*." The PIN is never echoed (unchanged, golden thread 1.5). **Status: OPEN for the structural fix:** PIN entry off chat, designed in the incident doc (a one-time signed link to an app.wapay.co.za page that reuses the `/api/app/pin` proof, golden thread 2.13, or WhatsApp Flows). It is a new money path and needs the founder's decision.
- **Guard (interim):** `tests/payout-incident-2026-10-28.test.mjs` asserts the exact PIN prompt.
- **Customer action:** change the PIN and delete the message that contains the old one.

## 148. "✅ … approved and handed to the bank rail" and "❌ … could not be completed by the bank rail" arrived in the same minute

- **Symptom (21:40 SAST):** two messages about the same R100, one with a green tick and one with a red cross. Elsewhere the same state reads "⏳ In progress". "Pay-out" and "withdrawal" name the same thing in alternate messages, and "bank rail" means nothing to a customer.
- **Root cause:** pay-out status wording is written in five places that drifted apart: `approvedCopy` (`lib/payout-approval.js`: ✅ on PENDING, which means only that the rail accepted it), `payoutOutcomeMessage` (`lib/payouts.js`), `handlePayoutStatus` (processor), the chat's in-progress line (`lib/payout-chat.js` `executeWithdraw`), and the web app's line (`lib/app-money.js`). In approval mode the founder's approval is the moment the request goes to the rail, so a rail failure seconds later contradicts a ✅ the customer has just read.
- **Fix (CHANGELOG 134):** `lib/payout-status-copy.js` holds the one vocabulary (`payoutPhase`, `onItsWayCopy`, `failedCopy`, `cancelledCopy`, `payoutLine`, `missingMoneyReply`, `openPayoutCopy`). `approvedCopy` says "⏳ *Approved and on its way.*" for a PENDING; the chat's in-progress line, the rejection, the transactions list and the web app's lines lost "bank rail". The paid message with its collection steps stays in `payoutOutcomeMessage`.
- **Guard:** `tests/payout-incident-2026-10-28.test.mjs` asserts the exact approval and failure copy, a ✅ only beside "paid", and no "bank rail", em dash, time words or "pay-out" in any of the copy; the chat and approval suites lock ⏳ for PENDING.

## 147. A second withdrawal was offered while the first was still waiting, and its confirmation hid the money set aside

- **Symptom (21:18 to 21:20 SAST):** the withdraw flow offered R228 to the customer's Capitec account (•••918), with "Total leaving your balance R244, Balance after R0". At that moment R116 (the R100 withdrawal plus its R16 fee) was already set aside for a withdrawal waiting for the founder's approval. The customer could not see that, and at 21:40 she was told about an R100 she did not recognise. Her balance was R360 the whole evening: R244 available plus R116 held.
- **Root cause:** (1) nothing limits a customer to one open withdrawal. `startWithdraw` never looks for an open pay-out, and neither does `requestPayoutCore`, so two intents can both be AWAITING_APPROVAL or PENDING at once. (2) `payoutBalances` reads only `availableCents`, and the confirmation's "Balance after" is that figure minus the total, so held money is silent. (3) `moneySituation` (`lib/money-situation.js`) lists only PENDING pay-outs, so a pay-out in AWAITING_APPROVAL (approval mode, CHANGELOG 129) is invisible to every gate that reads it. (4) The express fee (R16 instead of R8 within 2 hours of money arriving) gives a reason to split or retry, which makes overlapping withdrawals more likely.
- **Fix (CHANGELOG 134):** `findOpenPayout` treats INIT, AWAITING_APPROVAL, PENDING and REJECTING as open; NEEDS_OPERATOR waits for a person and must not block the customer, or the operator's own make-whole pay-out. `requestPayoutCore` refuses `OPEN_PAYOUT` after the replay and before any row, cent, hold or rail call, and does not report it as a new request. Migration `20261028_one_open_payout` adds a partial unique index on `ProviderRequest("accountId")` for those statuses; a P2002 on it becomes `OPEN_PAYOUT`. `startWithdraw` answers with `openPayoutCopy`: what is open, what is set aside, the balance split, and "cancel withdrawal" while it waits for approval. That runs `rejectPayout` with `by: 'customer'` and outcome `CANCELLED_BY_CUSTOMER`, and the chat sends the one reply. `moneySituation` and the R0 gate see AWAITING_APPROVAL, and `payoutBalances` returns `heldCents`. The web app answers 409 with the same copy.
- **Guard:** the processor replay (a second withdrawal while the R100 waits starts nothing and names it). The unit suite covers the refusal with nothing written, moved, held or sent, the replay still replaying, the index race, the chat's answer, the cancel, the money situation, and the migration naming the same statuses as the code.
- **Open question (to confirm):** where and when the R100 was requested. No request for R100 appears in her screenshots. The code allows only one origin, a PIN-approved chat or web request; the `PAYOUT_REQUEST` audit row for that reference says when.

## 146. A rejected pay-out told the customer "could not be completed by the bank rail" and nothing else

- **Symptom (21:40 SAST):** "❌ Your withdrawal of R100 by PayShap could not be completed by the bank rail (reference WP161EF3F64B2374). The full amount and the fee are back in your WaPay balance." The message gave no reason, no next step, no balance and no way to reach a person.
- **Root cause:** OTT's webhook sends a status code and a message. `finalisePayout` stores them on the pay-out row (`outcome: PROVIDER_<status>`, `lastMessage`), but `payoutOutcomeMessage` takes neither: one sentence covers every failure. `railAndSettle` keeps the code on an immediate failure but drops it from what it returns. The founder is not told when a pay-out he approved fails, and a code nobody has mapped raises no alert.
- **Production facts (read 29 October):** `FAILED`, outcome `PROVIDER_97`, OTT's message "Failed", nothing more. It was requested at 20:48:14 SAST, approved at 21:39:54 and failed at 21:40:54. OTT passed on no bank reason, so the specific cause needs OTT support. Still to confirm: whether the earlier R325 (`WPED6D478285A1E9`, paid) used the same destination and name.
- **Fix (CHANGELOG 134):** `lib/payout-reasons.js`, the rejection table as data: code or outcome to a reason, a next step that fits the method, a fault and a fee waiver, with a 97 refined by the bank's words when OTT gives them. `finalisePayout` and `railAndSettle` return `providerStatus`, `outcome` and `message`. `payoutOutcomeMessage` calls `failedCopy`, which states the reason, the money back, the balance (passed by the webhook) and the next step, ending "Reply *person* to talk to someone from WaPay" (`handlePersonAsk`). Every rail failure except an empty float (which has its own alert) sends `opsPayoutFailed` to the founder, masked, once per reference, flagged UNMAPPED when no row names the code.
- **Guard:** `tests/payout-incident-2026-10-28.test.mjs` checks every row's shape and plain words; 97 with and without the bank's words; an unknown code flagged; and approve, PENDING, the webhook's 97, the copy with the reason and the balance, and the alert built from masks. The reconcile, chat and wire suites lock the new failure wording.

## 145. "Nothing is coming in to my bank account" was answered with the withdraw menu

- **Symptom (22:13 SAST):** the customer wrote "Nothing is coming in to my bank account". The reply at 22:14 was the PayShap withdraw opener: "🏦 PayShap to your bank account: R16 fee (double the usual R8) … You have R420, so up to R404 … How much?" The complaint was not recognised and her last withdrawal was not looked up. The fee was doubled because of the R60 that reached her at 22:08.
- **Root cause:** `matchWithdrawAsk` (`lib/payout-chat.js`) treats `(?:to|into) my bank` as a withdraw word (BUGLOG #101, "lets do a withdrawal to my bank account"), so "coming in **to my bank** account" read as a request to withdraw. In `handlePostOnboarding` the withdraw matcher runs before `matchDepositStatusRequest`, so a sentence that matches both goes to the withdraw flow. Reproduced against `9da2068`: "did my withdrawal go through", "did my withdrawal to my capitec account go through" and "why did my withdrawal fail" all match `matchWithdrawAsk`. A missing-money complaint with no question word ("nothing came in", "I didn't receive my money", "my money did not arrive in my bank") matches neither pattern, so it reaches the AI, which must never state transaction status (constitution invariant 7).
- **Fix (CHANGELOG 134):** `lib/missing-money.js` `matchMissingMoney` covers complaints and withdrawal status questions. The processor checks it, with "cancel withdrawal" and "person", before the Pay agent and before the withdraw matcher. `handleMissingMoney` answers from the pay-out rows: `handlePayoutStatus` gives the last three withdrawals of 7 days through `missingMoneyReply`, with a PENDING row reconciled first; a deposit question goes to the deposit lookup. `matchWithdrawAsk` returns null for these sentences. The opportunistic reconcile hook leaves the ask to its handler.
- **Guard:** `tests/payout-incident-chat.test.mjs` sends her sentence through the real processor and asserts the answer: the R100 that didn't go through and why, the R325 paid, nothing on its way, R420, no flow started. Against `9da2068` it reproduces the 22:14 opener. Also the Pay agent pilot path, with no model call. `tests/payout-incident-2026-10-28.test.mjs` locks the matchers both ways and the hook order in the processor source.

## 144. Meta's second "Sending spam" strike: a receipt rail could still write first to numbers that never wrote to WaPay, and two strikes reached the founder six hours late as one more line in a daily RED

- **Symptom (Meta's e-mail, 26 October 2026):** "WhatsApp Business account WaPay has been identified as sending spam … the account may face restrictions or be disabled." The webhook had received the matching `account_update` event, `ACCOUNT_VIOLATION` / `SPAM`, at 20:04 UTC on 26 October, and an identical one at **20:03 UTC on 23 October** that nobody acted on. Quality rating RED without a break since 20 October, sending LIMITED, number CONNECTED. The first strike (#130) became a 30-day restriction 13 hours later (#136), lifted on review on 21 October.
- **What WaPay sent, 18 to 27 October** (inbound `wamid`s decoded to their sender, Meta's per-message billing pulses, `conversation_turns`, payment intents; scripts `~/Projects/wapay-ops/spam2-*.mjs`):
  - Since 23 October, 3,996 of the 4,053 messages WaPay recorded to account holders were replies within five minutes to that person's own message; the other 57 were 53 operator alerts to the founder's own number and 4 flow or receipt lines inside the customer's window. Customer messages that got three or more replies: 3 in five days. Reminders 0, marketing 0 (Meta billed no marketing).
  - **One business-initiated message to a stranger:** at 03:07 UTC on 26 October the payment-receipt UTILITY template went to the number typed as the payer on a link created during the 22 October stolen-card wave, whose card payment settled that night. That number has never written to WaPay. Meta billed it utility. On 21 and 22 October the same rail had tried 37 such numbers as plain text, which Meta dropped (131047 in 10 minutes, 131026 "not on WhatsApp" in 24 minutes on 22 October); BUGLOG #138's fix (248f531, 23 October) moved never-wrote payers onto the template, which delivers.
  - **Who WaPay talks to:** 86% of the minutes Meta billed from 23 to 26 October were `referral_conversion` (click-to-WhatsApp ad entries: 2,119 against 350 service). 1,839 ad conversations from 18 to 26 October (R3,806 spend; R629 and R578 on 23 and 24 October against about R400 before); 693 of 1,851 ad arrivals got one message and never wrote again; about 60% sent nothing beyond the ad's prefilled opener and button taps; ad arrivals wrote "Scam" (5), "Blocked"/"block" (3) and "stop" (3). 17 of the 28 frozen accounts wrote 177 times since 24 October and 22 customers asked about pay-outs during the pause; all were answered, none written to first.
- **Root cause:** (1) "WaPay never starts a conversation" was a rule in the docs, not in the rails. The payer receipt (any number typed on the pay page), `notifyCustomer` outside the window, the ops support template and the business nudge accepted any number; only the 24-hour window was checked, and a typed number is not consent. (2) Meta counts blocks and reports. With nothing else started by WaPay, the remaining source is the people WaPay answers: about 230 ad arrivals a day who did not know WaPay, kept coming while the rating was RED. Meta exposes no block or report data by API, so (2) is inferred from the billing mix, the timing and the words people sent; WhatsApp Manager's Account quality page shows Meta's reason.
- **Why the daily audit did not stop it or tell the founder in time:** it saw both strikes. The webhook stored each as a pulse row and did nothing else; the 02:00 compliance run read them six hours later (24 October 02:00, 27 October 02:01), went RED with `ACCOUNT_EVENT`, sent the e-mail (outcome not stored) and a WhatsApp line Meta delivered to the founder at 02:01 both days. But the verdict had read RED every day since 20 October (QUALITY_RED), the line was "WhatsApp compliance RED (date)" with the strike as a code mid-list, the e-mail subject was the same every day, it landed at 04:01 SAST next to the quality and audit lines and dozens of money-in and float alerts (69 money-in on 22 October), and the 24-hour window dropped the 23 October strike from the verdict by the 25th. It was a report, not a brake: nothing in it could refuse a send, and nothing said "act now". Quality was read once a day (the `phone_number_quality_update` webhook field is not subscribed).
- **Fix (CHANGELOG 133):** `lib/outbound-guard.js` (`businessInitiatedAllowed`): a number that never wrote to WaPay (no account, a pre-registered payer record without a message, or an unreadable answer) gets nothing on any rail outside a reply; each refusal is a `bi-refused:<purpose>` row. Wired into the payer receipt (after the pre-registration record, before any rail), the requester notice's template rails, `notifyCustomer` outside the window, the ops reply after the window (force does not override it) and the business nudge. The payer gets the receipt by tapping "Get my receipt" (their own message opens the chat); the pay page's fine print now says so. `lib/meta-alerts.js`: `metaEventAlert` alerts on every Meta account event at arrival, by e-mail and WhatsApp, "🛑 ACTION NOW" for enforcement, with the 7-day count and the billing mix; `qualityWatch` reads quality, tier and status every ten minutes from the webhook and alerts on any change; `strangerCheck` checks every status Meta bills as utility, marketing or authentication against the database and alerts plus counts `stranger-<category>` when the recipient never wrote. Every alert stores `alert-out:<kind>:mail-<0|1>:wa-<n>`. `lib/meta-compliance.js`: `META_STRIKE_<minute>` over 7 days with dates (each strike is new), `SENT_TO_STRANGER` RED, refusals reported, `AD_TRAFFIC_WHILE_RED` AMBER, new reasons first, an e-mail subject that names what is new, the e-mail outcome stored. `/api/internal/meta-status?node=<id>` reads what a Meta id in an e-mail is.
- **Guard:** `tests/spam-strike-guard.test.mjs` (the guard's definition of "wrote"; every file that can send a template or Direct Send is listed and the business-initiated ones call the guard; the receipt order; the alert at arrival, once per event, outcome on record; quality baseline, fall and recovery; the stranger check; strikes for 7 days with a new code per strike; the e-mail subject; the pay page copy), plus never-wrote cases in `notify`, `ops-reply`, `payer-registration` and `payer-preregister` tests.
- **Lesson:** a check that is RED every day is not an alarm. Meta's enforcement needs its own alert the minute it lands, and "only people who wrote to us" has to be enforced where the message leaves, not assumed by the caller.

## 142. A frozen account could still move its money: paying someone's link from a balance is a send the freeze did not check

- **Symptom (found 2026-10-26 in the founder's morning check):** on 25 October at 17:27 and 17:32 SAST two of the 28 frozen accounts (•••0522, •••8951) paid R285 each from their balances into an account opened that hour (•••8207) by paying its payment links ("Just money", "rent"). •••8207 tried to withdraw at 17:34, met the pause, and at 17:37 paid R570 back into •••0522 through •••0522's own link ("Cash").

## 141. The test suite read the production database through Prisma's own .env loading

- **Symptom (found 2026-10-24 while shipping the global pay-out hold):** 29 withdraw-flow tests failed the moment the hold row existed in production. They had never set a database: `node --test` passes no `DATABASE_URL`, but Prisma Client loads the project's `.env` by itself, and the fast copy's `.env` holds the production connection string. Every test that called code with a default client (`lib/prisma.js`, `getPrisma()` in `packages/domain`, the WhatsApp package's template catalogue, seven VAS routes with their own client) read production, took pooler connections and depended on production state.
- **Root cause:** a convenience default (Prisma's automatic `.env`) met a fast copy whose `.env` is the operator's production toolbox. Nothing marked a test process as different.
- **Fix (CHANGELOG 125):** under `node --test` (`NODE_TEST_CONTEXT`) every client points at `TEST_DATABASE_URL` or a closed local port; the VAS routes use the shared client; `pnpm test` loads `.env.test` when present. The suite passes with no database at all.
- **Guard:** `tests/test-db-guard.test.mjs` asserts both shared clients refuse at the closed port and that every `new PrismaClient(` in the app carries the guard. Lesson: a test run must not be able to reach production by default; isolation belongs in the client, not in how someone remembers to start the tests.

## 140. Stolen cards were cashed out through payment links: R20,466 with fake payer details on 22 October, R43,551 paid out before PayFast stopped card payments

- **Symptom (found 2026-10-24 from PayFast's transaction export):** from 22:22 SAST on 22 October PayFast accepted no typed-card payment; only Apple Pay went through (R107, then the founder's R20). The export showed why: 61 pay-link payments with fake payer details (34 throwaway e-mails at example.com, drsonny.org and keyboard-mash domains, made-up cells such as 073 123 4567, one cell paying 10 links in an hour), R20,466, to 16 accounts opened that day, none from the ad; plus 13 payments, R36,300, where people paid their own links by card (the R3,000 pattern; one customer said the cards were international).
- **Money:** the 24 accounts behind those payments were credited R56,342 net; R43,551 had been paid out to banks or cash by 24 October; R10,865 was still in their balances; about R11,300 circulated between them by sends.
- **Root cause:** a payment link takes any card with any payer details and the money can leave the same day: no velocity limit per payer or per new account, no hold on card-funded money, KYC only from R3,000 in one pay-out or R10,000 a day, so many R300 payments and small pay-outs stayed under every line.
- **Fix:** 28 accounts frozen for pay-outs and sends on 24 October (founder's instruction; list in `~/Projects/wapay-ops/frozen-2026-10-24.json`; receiving stays open); founder to confirm PayFast's block and discuss chargebacks and any reserve.
- **Pay-outs paused for everyone (24 October, founder's instruction):** the global pay-out hold (CHANGELOG 124) has been on since 23:07 UTC on 23 October. It stays on until the founder reopens pay-outs (`POST /api/internal/payout-hold` with `held: false`, `by`, `reason`). Before reopening: the OTT float reconciled against the bank transfers, PayFast's block, chargebacks and any reserve settled, and the guard below decided and built.
- **Guard (open, founder's decision):** card-funded money in an account younger than seven days waits 72 hours, or needs the ID check, before it can be paid out; refuse a payer number that is the recipient's own; cap card payments per payer and per new account per hour. Apple Pay still works at PayFast, so the same ring can return through new accounts until one of these is in.

## 138. Paid-link receipts to payers who never wrote to WaPay were dropped by Meta while WaPay counted them as sent

And when a .NET API echoes its parsed request, read the echo: every default it filled in is a value it hashed.

## 32. Admin console + Didit KYC — 27-agent adversarial review (caught pre-ship 2026-08-28)

The whole admin/KYC surface was reviewed by a 27-finding adversarial workflow BEFORE it saw
prod traffic. The load-bearing catches, all fixed in the same push:

- **CRITICAL — admin allowlist matched last-9-digits only.** `isAdminMsisdn`/`adminAccount`
  reduced numbers to a 9-digit tail, so a foreign/VoIP WhatsApp number sharing an admin's

  full SA 27-form (`normSa`), resolve the account by exact full-number match, and fail closed

- **HIGH — admin OTP shared the `otp_codes` table with customer money-flow OTPs.** Admin
  verify consumed the newest live row of EITHER flow, burning a customer's onboarding code.
  **Fix:** admin codes are namespaced `adm:`+hash and every admin query filters on the

- **HIGH — profile JSON lost-update race.** Every profile writer did read-modify-write on the
  whole column; a KYC merge and a language write on the next message clobbered each other.

- **HIGH — KYC webhook could permanently lose the customer notification** (gated on a

## 28. OTT Payout client: amount hash/wire mismatch + two double-spend paths (caught pre-launch)

- **Symptom (adversarial review 2026-08-26, before any live credential existed):** three defects in the new payout client. (a) **BLOCKER** — the hash was computed over the 2dp string `"50.00"` but the body sent `Number("50.00")` → `50`, so OTT would recompute its hash over `"50"` and **every round-rand withdrawal** would fail with status 2 Invalid Hash. My own test asserted `amount === 50`, i.e. it *locked the bug in* and made a green suite meaningless. (b) a transport failure/timeout **threw**, giving the caller no settlement class — if a caller treated "threw" as failure and released the hold, the customer could respend money OTT had already paid. (c) status `3` (duplicate reference) mapped to RELEASE, but with our deterministic epoch-free reference a duplicate means an **earlier attempt already reached OTT and may have succeeded** — releasing double-spends.
- **Root cause:** (a) serialising the amount twice, once for the hash and once for the wire, in different types; (b)/(c) treating "no/negative answer" as "nothing happened" — the classic indeterminate-payment fallacy.
- **Fix:** the wire amount is now the exact hashed 2dp string; transport failures RETURN `{outcome:'TRANSPORT_INDETERMINATE', settlement:'PENDING', reconcileRequired:true}` instead of throwing; status `3` is PENDING+reconcile. The caller contract ("never release on PENDING; after reconcileRequired call getPaymentStatus, never re-issue performPayout") is documented in the module header and `docs/OTT_PAYOUT_API.md`.
- **Guard:** `tests/ott-payout.test.mjs` asserts the wire amount equals the hashed amount as a STRING across 5 amount shapes incl. round rands, drives a real transport error to prove the PENDING outcome, and pins `3` → reconcile. The two OTT golden vectors remain pinned so the crypto can't drift.

## 27. Directed-request relationship gate was self-populatable (phishing surface)

- **Symptom (re-review 2026-08-25, pre-deploy):** the rebuilt directed-request gate ("please pay me R50 from <name/number>") required the target to be the requester's saved beneficiary — but a beneficiary is created UNCONDITIONALLY by sharing a WhatsApp contact card (`rememberBeneficiary`, no money, no target consent). So an attacker could save any victim's number via a contact-card share, then push an unsolicited (label-spoofable) "pay request" nudge into that stranger's WaPay chat and read the requester-side response as a membership-enumeration oracle. The CORE harm (cross-user state plant / auto-pay) was already closed; this was the residual delivery+oracle surface.
- **Fix:** the gate is now a real PRIOR MONEY MOVEMENT — `hasPriorSendTo` (a `PendingGift` from the requester to that recipient exists), applied on BOTH the number and name branches. A prior send is money-backed and cannot be forged for free, and the recipient already received value from the sender (benign). Plus: `safeRequesterLabel` gained a system/authority denylist (wapay/support/admin/…→ neutral label), and the informational nudge is no longer written into the payer's conversation history (kept out of their AI-context window).
- **Guard:** `tests/founder-feedback-0825.test.mjs` requires ≥2 `hasPriorSendTo` gates in the resolver, asserts `isSavedBeneficiary` is gone from it, and locks the denylist + no-history + no-state + no-money properties of delivery.

## 26. Paid-request notifications were one-shot — a lost invocation lost them forever

- **Symptom (founder live test `PRMDCUQA`, R20 card payment, 2026-08-25):** the payment was captured perfectly — payer number stored, request marked PAID, requester credited — but NEITHER the requester's "you've been paid" nor the payer's receipt ever arrived on WhatsApp.
